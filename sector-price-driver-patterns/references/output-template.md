# Output template

The reader wants **the explanation of what moved the group**, not the data that produced it. Steps 1–5 generate a lot of working data (return tables, move counts, daily attributions). Almost none of it belongs in the output. Every number that appears must be the **evidence for a specific conclusion**; if a number doesn't change how the reader understands the window, cut it.

## Hard rules
- **Length:** about 350–500 words. If it's longer, synthesize more.
- **No per-stock tables** and no lists of returns or dates for their own sake. The only table is the layer table (one row per layer, max ~8 rows), with its median caption.
- **No chronology.** Don't walk through the window day by day. Collapse synchronized sell-offs into one finding (e.g. "5 group-wide sell-offs, all macro or sentiment-driven").
- **No methodology narration** ("I screened…", "Checking prices…"). Never output working notes or tool-progress text.
- **Each finding = claim + 1–2 decisive data points + exception** (if any, in a phrase).
- **No trade content:** no ideas, setup, positioning verdicts, catalysts or target-price upside (SKILL.md Output).
- **Data gaps:** mention only gaps that weaken a conclusion, in one line.
- Offer the full data (returns table, move log) at the end in one sentence; don't include it.

## Structure

**Scope:** [theme] · [window] · [region]

**Bottom line:** 2–3 sentences: what drove the group, the main pattern, and which layers led and lagged. Bold the key conclusion.

## Key findings
3–4 numbered patterns, each 2–3 lines, strongest first. Each pattern must be tested against the returns data; say so briefly by naming the exception.

## How each layer moved
*Reads vs the universe median ([x]%); In line = within ±2 pp.*
| Layer | Median return | Read | Main driver (one line of evidence) |
Reads: **Led**, **Lagged**, **In line**; add **— divergent** where management tone and price moved apart.

## Price vs fundamentals
1–3 lines, one per divergence Step 4 found: what management said, what the price did. If none: one line saying no layer's earnings commentary diverged from its price move.

**Latest month:** whether the main pattern **held**, **faded** or **reversed** in the final month, with the deciding number.

*Caveat line (only if a data gap affects a conclusion, or to count sweep and re-score additions: "N names added by sector sweep", "N names added by re-score"). An open coverage item — a layer without its synthesize research read, or a large-move log not paged to `truncated = false` — is not a caveat: the run is partial, so name the open items and resume next turn; the final output is sent only with them closed. Analysis, not investment advice. Full returns table and move log available on request.*

## Example (AI infrastructure, Jun–Sep 2026)

**Scope:** AI infrastructure · Jun 22 → Sep 22, 2026 · global

**Bottom line:** The quarter **derated crowded supply-chain leaders on rates and positioning, not weaker demand**. The market paid for proven AI revenue and punished spending and financing: **servers and monetizing hyperscalers led; memory, semicap, optical and power lagged**.

## Key findings
1. **June leaders were derated regardless of fundamentals.** Memory, optical, semicap and power fell 16–25% (layer medians); 9 of 28 names peaked on day one of the window. Exception: Dell and Super Micro rose on very large beats.
2. **Beats were sold; capex was treated as a cost.** Eight names fell 5–17% on beats or raised guidance, and Alphabet fell 7% on a record quarter because it raised capex. Monetizers won: Microsoft +36% on Azure, Meta +31% on its AI assistant, versus Oracle −15%.
3. **The supply chain trades as a factor.** About half of large moves in memory, optical and semicap had no company-specific driver, versus 14% for hyperscalers **(Distilla)**. Rates (10-year near 5%, a Fed hike on Sep 16) set direction for the chain **(Web)**.
4. **Financing and input costs marked the losers.** Equity and convert raises hit Ciena, Celestica and Oracle; optical margins were squeezed by indium phosphide costs. Exception: Lumentum held up.

## How each layer moved
*Reads vs the universe median (−12%); In line = within ±2 pp.*
| Layer | Median return | Read | Main driver |
|---|---|---|---|
| Servers | +28% | **Led** | Very large beats at Dell and Super Micro |
| Hyperscalers | +9% | **Led** | Rewarded only with visible monetization; capex raisers sold |
| Power | −16% | **Lagged** | Group moves on rates; rising policy risk |
| Optical | −19% | **Lagged** | Equity raises and an indium phosphide cost squeeze |
| Memory | −22% | **Lagged — divergent** | Half of large moves macro while all three majors reported rising prices |
| Semicap | −25% | **Lagged — divergent** | Factor selling while 2026 equipment-spend forecasts were raised |

## Price vs fundamentals
- **Memory:** fell ~22% while all three majors reported rising prices and described HBM as tight through 2028 — the widest tone-vs-price gap in the universe.
- **Semicap:** fell ~25% while 2026 equipment-spend forecasts were raised; JPMorgan saw no capex downturn.

**Latest month:** **The split widened.** Servers, AMD and Meta ran 25–35% in September while second-tier optical kept making lower lows.

*Analysis, not investment advice. Full returns table and move log available on request.*
