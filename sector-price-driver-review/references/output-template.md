# Output template

The reader wants **conclusions they can trade on**, not the data that produced them. Steps 1–5 generate a lot of working data (return tables, move counts, daily attributions). Almost none of it belongs in the output. Every number that appears must be the **evidence for a specific conclusion**; if a number doesn't change what the reader would do, cut it.

## Hard rules
- **Length:** about 350–500 words. If it's longer, synthesize more.
- **No per-stock tables** and no lists of returns, targets or dates for their own sake. The only table is the layer verdict table (one row per layer, max ~8 rows).
- **No chronology.** Don't walk through the quarter day by day. Collapse synchronized sell-offs into one finding (e.g. "5 group-wide sell-offs, all macro or sentiment-driven").
- **No methodology narration** ("I screened…", "Checking prices…"). Never output working notes or tool-progress text.
- **Each finding = claim + 1–2 decisive data points + exception** (if any, in a phrase).
- **Each idea = thesis + evidence + catalyst + invalidation**, one short line each.
- **Data gaps:** mention only gaps that weaken a conclusion, in one line.
- Offer the full data (returns table, move log) at the end in one sentence; don't include it.

## Structure

**Scope:** [theme] · [window] · [horizon] · [region]

**Bottom line:** 2–3 sentences: what drove the group, the main pattern, and the best setup now. Bold the key conclusion.

## Key findings
3–4 numbered patterns, each 2–3 lines, strongest first. Each pattern must be tested against the returns data; say so briefly by naming the exception.

## Where the opportunity is
| Layer | Verdict | Why (one line of evidence) |
Verdicts: **Oversold**, **Crowded**, **Mixed**, **Avoid**, **Neutral**.

## Trade ideas
**Long** (up to 3) and **Avoid** (up to 3), each one name or one named pair (e.g. Samsung / SK hynix) — never a basket. Format per idea:
**[Name/basket] — [one-phrase thesis]**
- Evidence: 1–2 data points that make the case.
- Catalyst: event (date). Invalidation: what would prove it wrong.

**Watch this week:** one item and why it matters to the ideas above.

*Caveat line (only if a data gap affects a conclusion, or to count sweep and re-score additions: "N names added by sector sweep", "N names added by re-score"). An open coverage item — a layer without its synthesize research read, or a large-move log not paged to `truncated = false` — is not a caveat: the run is partial, so name the open items and resume next turn; the final output is sent only with them closed. Analysis, not investment advice. Full returns table and move log available on request.*

## Example (AI infrastructure, Jun–Sep 2026)

**Scope:** AI infrastructure · Jun 22 → Sep 22, 2026 · 2–6 weeks · global

**Bottom line:** The quarter **derated crowded supply-chain leaders on rates and positioning, not weaker demand**. The market paid for proven AI revenue and punished spending and financing. **Best setup into October earnings: oversold memory and semicap, where fundamentals are still improving; avoid names now priced for perfection.**

## Key findings
1. **June leaders were derated regardless of fundamentals.** Memory, optical, semicap and power fell 16–25% (layer medians); 13 of 39 names peaked on day one of the window. Exception: Dell and Super Micro rose on very large beats.
2. **Beats were sold; capex was treated as a cost.** Eight names fell 5–17% on beats or raised guidance, and Alphabet fell 7% on a record quarter because it raised capex. Monetizers won: Microsoft +36% on Azure, Meta +31% on its AI assistant, versus Oracle −15%.
3. **The supply chain trades as a factor.** About half of large moves in memory, optical and semicap had no company-specific driver, versus 14% for hyperscalers. Rates (10-year near 5%, a Fed hike on Sep 16) set direction for the chain.
4. **Financing and input costs marked the losers.** Equity and convert raises hit Ciena, Celestica and Oracle; optical margins were squeezed by indium phosphide costs. Exception: Lumentum held up.

## Where the opportunity is
| Layer | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Memory | **Oversold** | Down ~22% while all three majors report rising prices; HBM seen tight through 2028 |
| Semicap | **Oversold** | Down ~25% while 2026 equipment-spend forecasts were raised |
| Hyperscalers | **Mixed** | Rewarded only with visible monetization |
| Servers, AMD, Meta | **Crowded** | At or above consensus targets after 25–35% one-month runs |
| Optical (2nd tier) | **Avoid** | Still making lower lows; high beta and input-cost squeeze |
| Power | **Neutral** | Solid demand, rising policy risk |

## Trade ideas
**Long**
**SK hynix / Samsung — memory catch-up**
- Evidence: down 22–37% despite rising prices; the widest fundamentals-vs-price gap in the universe.
- Catalyst: Micron read-through (Sep 30), own reports (Oct 27–28). Invalidation: Micron guides margins or pricing lower.

**Tokyo Electron / Lam — semicap rerating**
- Evidence: down 25–32% while equipment forecasts rose; JPMorgan sees no capex downturn.
- Catalyst: TSMC capex commentary (Oct 15), Lam (Oct 21). Invalidation: TSMC trims 2027 capex.

**Avoid**
**Meta, AMD, Bloom — priced for perfection**
- Evidence: at or above targets after 31–37% runs, in a tape that sells beats.
- Catalyst: earnings Oct 27–Nov 3. Invalidation: guidance large enough to force target resets.

**Oracle — financing risk**
- Evidence: BBB− rating, a $20B equity program, and one customer at ~50% of backlog.
- Catalyst: rates. Invalidation: clear funding progress.

**Watch this week:** **Micron's report (Sep 30)**. It confirms or breaks the memory and semicap longs.
